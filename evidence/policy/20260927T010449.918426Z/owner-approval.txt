D1 — APPROVE WITH EXPLICIT SCOPE

Adopt the proposed explicit-chain event identity, source-version
identity, persistent alert-key/attempt/acknowledgement requirements,
same-key retry behavior, and assessment-only retention policy.

Keep storage receipts distinct from delivery acknowledgements.
Do not claim duplicate-free delivery without the required receiver
contract and execution evidence.

An incomplete or disputed chain blocks automatic alerting, not retention
of the input or an explicitly uncertain candidate for review.

D2 — APPROVE WITH AMENDMENTS

Adopt suppression of operationally unchanged revisions and explicit
materiality adjudication for other changes.

Clarify initial versus update alerts:

- If a resolved event has no prior initial-alert record or attempt,
  its first material and actionable decision may create the initial alert.
- If an initial-alert record already exists, a qualifying material
  revision produces a linked update.
- An unacknowledged initial attempt reuses its existing key; replay
  must not create another initial alert.

For the bounded same-ID/different-source-content case:
retain the conflicting capture as evidence, preserve the last accepted
snapshot, and require review before the changed content can authorize
alerting. Do not automatically overwrite, merge, discard, or reconcile
the conflict.

Automatic reconciliation of that conflict remains outside the
implemented storage scope. This is a specified refusal boundary,
not a claim that a complete source-version service exists.

D3 — APPROVE WITH REPRODUCIBILITY CLARIFICATION

Adopt the explicit reference clock, recorded source-time semantics,
history-gap deferral, reconciliation decisions, historical-only boundary,
and human review of unresolved actionability.

Define order-independent final-view checks using the same accepted
source versions, normalized facts, reference time, and approved
adjudications. Do not expect independently regenerated stochastic model
outputs to be identical.

Historical delivery attempts remain historical; order independence of
the final view does not imply identical real-time delivery histories.

Preserve the distinction between historical label evaluation and
permission to issue a current alert. Do not rescore old labels against
today's clock.

D4 — APPROVE

Adopt immutable decision identity and retrieval of the context actually
available at decision time.

Later information and corrections create linked decisions. They must
not be retroactively presented as facts known during an earlier decision.

Preserve missing-history observations as missingness, rather than
backfilling later facts into an old explanation.

Keep assessment retention bounded as proposed; make no production
retention or complete historical-service implementation claim.

D5 — APPROVE WITH SCORING CORRECTION

Adopt explicit uncertain/error dispositions, reason codes, retained
input evidence, and preservation of the last valid snapshot.

Replace the blanket "unscorable behavior evidence" treatment:

Classification is unscorable only where the necessary semantic result
is unavailable. Structural, persistence, uncertainty, and failure-handling
requirements remain evaluable when their required observations exist.

Keep application dispositions distinct from harness PASS/FAIL/ERROR/
UNKNOWN statuses and execution-domain classifications.

A successful API response with an unusable verdict must not silently
become a valid negative or "small impact" decision under the new policy.

D6 — APPROVE WITH ORACLE AND AUTHORIZATION SEPARATION

Adopt the proposed bounded human-adjudicated materiality policy and
source-grounded optional-field treatment.

The supplied 14 labels remain an evaluation oracle only. They do not
authorize runtime alerts and must not be supplied as the implementation's
answer to the classification problem.

For the assessment's alerting policy, require recorded approval and
satisfied identity, history, and actionability conditions. Model proposals
or a known test label are not approval.

Strong-signal characteristics remain evidence to weigh, not an all-fields
checklist. Do not invent numeric cutoffs or convert percentages into
absolute volume without the necessary source basis.

Approve prospective binding use of the narrowly described annotated
field checks and unchanged-operational supersede checks only within
their documented scope and preconditions.

Before applying that scope, verify that included annotations have
traceable source support. Unsupported annotations remain unapproved or
not evaluated and must be listed. This approval is not a claim that
the owner personally audited every annotation.

Do not expand narrow checks into proof of complete field extraction,
geographic relevance, absolute volume accuracy, or real material-update
handling.
